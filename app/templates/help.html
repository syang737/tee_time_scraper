How it works

Weekend tee times at these courses get booked out almost as soon as they're released. They come back only when somebody cancels — at a random moment, and usually gone again within minutes. You'd never catch that by refreshing the booking page.

So this checks for you, every 30 seconds, and buzzes your phone the instant something you'd actually want opens up. Two things to set up, both quick.

One thing worth saying plainly: this never books anything for you. It has no card and no course login. It tells you a slot is free — you tap the notification and book it yourself, the normal way.

1 Getting the notifications

Alerts don't come by text or email. They come through a free app called ntfy (say it "notify"). No account, no signup, no cost.

  1. Install ntfy on your phone.
    Search "ntfy" in the iPhone App Store or on Google Play. There's also a browser version if you'd rather not install anything.
  2. Open it and tap the + button.
    It'll ask you to "subscribe to a topic".
  3. Type in your topic name, exactly. {% if ntfy_topic %}
    Unless you've been given your own, it's this one:

    {{ ntfy_topic }}

    {% else %}
    Whoever set this up will give you one, or you can pick your own when you create a watch.
    {% endif %}
    Leave the server as the default (ntfy.sh) and tap Subscribe.
  4. Check it actually works.
    Come back here and press the button below. Your phone should buzz within a second or two. If it doesn't, the topic name usually has a typo in it — it has to match character for character.
A topic is just a secret word. Anyone who knows it can read your alerts, and anyone who doesn't, can't find it. So don't use something guessable like golf, and don't post it anywhere public. That's the whole security model — it's why there's no password or account to manage.

2 Setting up a watch

A watch is a saved description of the tee time you'd happily take. Something like "any of the three courses, Saturday or Sunday, before 10:30am, room for at least two of us."

You write that down once. The app then checks all three courses every 30 seconds and only bothers you when something matches everything you asked for. A 2pm slot won't wake you up if you said mornings.

What you choose

⛳ Courses
One, two, or all three. Most people tick all three — more chances.
📅 Days
Which days of the week, and how far ahead to look. Saturday and Sunday over the next 14 days covers "this weekend or next".
🕔 Time window
Earliest and latest you'd tee off. Leave both blank if you'd take anything.
👤 Players
The minimum number of open spots. Set 2 if there are two of you, so you don't get alerted about a slot with only one space left.
Holes
9, 18, or either.

What happens then

  1. A matching slot opens up, usually because somebody cancelled.
  2. Your phone buzzes within about 30 seconds.
  3. You tap the notification and it opens that course's booking page, already on the right day.
  4. You book it. Quickly — other people want it too.

Worth knowing

You'll get a reminder
If a slot is still free after 10 minutes, you get nudged again, in case you missed the first one. Once somebody books it, the nudging stops.
You won't get spammed
Each tee time only alerts you once. If it gets taken and later freed up again, that counts as new and you'll hear about it.
Pause instead of deleting
Not playing for a few weeks? Hit Pause on the watch and it stops alerting without losing the settings. Resume when you want it back.
Sharing with friends
Each watch can point at its own ntfy topic, so several people can use this and everyone only hears about their own. Put your own topic in when you create the watch, and name the watch after yourself so it's obvious whose is whose.
Nothing arriving?
Open the watch and press Send test to this topic. If the test arrives but real alerts don't, your criteria are probably just stricter than what's coming free — try widening the time window.
